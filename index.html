Roughly 2 to 5 percent of graded carcasses make USDA Prime in a given year. Most of it gets bought by steakhouses before it ever reaches a retail case. This one did not get away.

A

Maturity matters too

Grading combines marbling with carcass maturity (A through E, based on bone and cartilage ossification). Prime is only available to A and B maturity. An old cow with gorgeous marbling still will not grade Prime.

Fat

Is flavor, and also water

Intramuscular fat melts around 105 to 130°F, right in the range where a steak is cooking. It lubricates the muscle fiber bundles, carries fat-soluble flavor compounds, and makes the meat read as juicier even when it is technically losing moisture.
